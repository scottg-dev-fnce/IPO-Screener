# Defense Technology & Aerospace — Scoring Framework

SIC 3812 (Defense Electronics) / 3761 (Guided Missiles). Observed: HawkEye 360, Inc. (HAWK, PASS 63.0); The Elmet Group (ELMT, CONDITIONAL 62.2); Arxis, Inc. (ARXS, CONDITIONAL 58.5).

## The Defense Tech Pattern

Strong BMQ and MCP (government contracts = visible revenue, mission-critical stickiness) but VA almost always the killer. Defense tech companies IPO with valuations that embed significant government contract optionality premium — and the public market doesn't always pay that premium.

HawkEye: BMQ=88, MCP=82 (both high) — but VA=32 (3.2/10) triggered RF-07A (EV/Revenue >50% above sector median). PASS driven entirely by valuation.

## Government Revenue: Signal and Risk

Government customers provide:
- Long-term contract visibility (IDIQ, OTA, multi-year awards)
- Mission-critical stickiness (switching costs are extreme)
- Revenue predictability (appropriations cycles are predictable, if slow)

But government revenue introduces:
- **RF-02 customer concentration risk** — when DoD/NRO/NSA is 30-50% of revenue, the concentration flag fires even with a "sticky" government customer
- **Appropriations risk** — continuing resolution (CR) or budget sequestration can delay contract awards
- **Classification risk** — classified programs cannot be described in the S-1, limiting the public investor's ability to diligence the pipeline
- **ITAR/EAR risk** — export controls limit total addressable market for international expansion; flag RF-12 if company has foreign market expansion as a core growth thesis

## Valuation: The Common Failure Mode

Defense tech companies frequently price at:
- EV/Revenue premiums that embed large program win assumptions
- EBITDA multiples that don't account for the high R&D spend required to win next-gen programs

The VA dimension almost always scores 4.0-6.0 unless:
- The company is genuinely profitable (GAAP earnings) AND priced reasonably vs. peers (Booz Allen, SAIC, Leidos)
- Or the IPO is priced at a discount to established defense primes

**Benchmark multiples (2026):** Defense primes (LMT, RTX, NOC) at 12-18x EBITDA, 1-2x Revenue. Pure defense tech / dual-use at 3-7x Revenue depending on growth rate.

## Accounting in Defense Tech (RF-25 Risk)

- **ELMT scored RF-25** (accounting quality score ≤5) — common in defense contractors due to: percentage-of-completion revenue recognition, cost-type contract accounting, and complex IP licensing to government entities
- Always pull accounting notes on: (1) how contract revenue is recognized (milestone vs. POC vs. time-based); (2) whether R&D is expensed or capitalized; (3) how government-funded development is treated
- If revenue recognition is POC (percentage of completion): watch for "estimates" that inflate revenue recognition ahead of actual deliverables

## Management Risk (RF-08)

Defense company CEOs from government (former generals, SES civilians) sometimes lack:
- Capital markets experience (first public company CEO)
- Commercial pricing acumen (used to cost-plus, not fixed-price contracts)

Flag RF-08 if CEO has <2 years of private sector commercial experience before this role.

## What Drives CONDITIONAL vs. PASS in Defense Tech

- **PASS:** VA ≤4.0 + 2+ additional HIGH flags (valuation + governance or regulatory)
- **CONDITIONAL:** VA 5.0-6.5, government revenue diversified across 3+ agencies, clear commercial dual-use strategy
- **CONDITIONAL_LIGHT:** Profitable GAAP, multiple agency customers, priced at or below comparable defense primes
- **UNDERWRITE:** Profitable, diversified across DoD + commercial, strong FCF, priced at discount — rare in defense tech IPOs
