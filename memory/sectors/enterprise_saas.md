# Enterprise SaaS — Valuation Framework & UNDERWRITE Criteria

SIC 7372 (Prepackaged Software). Observed: Helix AI Corp (HLXAI, UNDERWRITE 78.0).

## UNDERWRITE Checklist (all four should be met)

| Metric | Threshold | Helix AI | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rule of 40 | ≥50 | 74 | Revenue growth % + FCF margin % |
| Net Revenue Retention (NRR) | ≥130% | 138% | Expansion within existing base |
| Gross Margin | ≥70% | 74.2% | Software-level margin confirms scalability |
| Syndicate quality | Goldman/MS lead | Goldman/MS | Tier-1 bilateral = strong book confidence |

A Rule of 40 ≥70 puts the company in the top decile of software IPOs. Below 40 = CONDITIONAL range regardless of growth rate.

## Valuation Framework

- **Primary metric:** EV/Revenue (not EV/EBITDA — pre-profitability SaaS valued on revenue multiple)
- **Secondary metric:** Forward EV/Revenue or EV/Gross Profit
- **Sector median (2026):** approximately 10-12x NTM Revenue for high-growth SaaS
- **Premium justified when:** NRR >140%, growth >50% YoY, gross margin >75%, clear path to Rule of 40 >50 on a forward basis
- Helix AI: 11.7x EV/Revenue vs 10.2x sector median — modest 15% premium, justified by Rule of 40 of 74

## Red Flag Risk Profile for SaaS

- **RF-06 (Governance):** Dual-class common at IPO is expected and normal in software; assess track record rather than structure
  - `proven_operator` or `emerging_operator` track record → manageable deduction
  - `first_time_public_ceo` on a dual-class = elevated risk
- **RF-03 (Revenue Quality):** Watch A/R vs revenue growth ratio; aggressive non-GAAP adjustments (capitalizing S&M as "customer acquisition cost") are common and worth flagging
- **RF-15 (Product Concentration):** Single-product SaaS companies are high-risk if product is early-stage; diversified platform is safer
- **RF-07B (EV/EBITDA growth mismatch):** EV/EBITDA >25x on GAAP-loss + <30% growth = flag. Fast-growing SaaS (>50% growth) generally avoids this trap.

## Management Assessment for SaaS

Most important signal in SaaS: **CEO's prior software exit experience**. One prior public company CEO or one prior material exit (IPO or strategic acquisition of a venture-backed software company) = `proven_operator` or high-confidence `emerging_operator`. No prior software leadership = flag even with a strong product.

## What Kills a SaaS UNDERWRITE

- NRR declining below 120% (retention is deteriorating)
- Growth rate falling below 30% with no clear re-acceleration driver
- Gross margin below 65% at scale (indicates infrastructure intensity not typical of software)
- Single customer >25% of revenue (RF-02 concentration)
- Customer count too small (<50 enterprise customers) for institutional comfort
- Rule of 40 below 30 with no credible path to improvement
