# Clinical-Stage Biotech — UNDERWRITE Criteria & Risk Framework

SIC 2836 (Pharmaceutical Preparations) or 2835/2836. Observed: Novacyte Therapeutics (NVCT, UNDERWRITE 77.3, allogeneic CAR-T).

## UNDERWRITE Criteria

The bar is high. All of the following should be present:

1. **Clinical data that differentiates** — Phase 2 or Phase 2b data showing meaningful improvement over standard of care or best available therapy. Phase 1 alone is almost never sufficient for UNDERWRITE.
2. **Regulatory validation** — Breakthrough Therapy Designation, Fast Track, or Priority Review from FDA is strong signal. Without it, timeline risk is higher.
3. **Validated partnership** — a Big Pharma or large cap licensing deal or collaboration with milestone payments provides: (a) third-party validation of the science, (b) non-dilutive capital to extend runway, (c) commercial infrastructure access.
4. **Management track record** — CEO with prior biotech exit (IPO or acquisition). CFO with prior Phase 3 capital management experience. Both matter.
5. **Syndicate quality** — JPM / Goldman / Leerink / SVB Leerink on the cover page signals institutional biotech investor acceptance.

Novacyte met all five: 68% CR at 6 months in r/r DLBCL (Phase 2b), Breakthrough Therapy Designation, Pfizer collaboration with milestone revenue, CEO Dr. Vasquez (2 prior exits), JPM/Goldman/Leerink lead.

## Valuation Framework

- **Primary metric:** rNPV of pipeline (risk-adjusted NPV discounting for clinical failure probability)
- **Secondary:** EV / Peak Sales estimate for lead asset
- **Industry failure rates:** Phase 1→2: ~60% success; Phase 2→3: ~45% success; Phase 3→approval: ~65% success. Cumulative PoS from Phase 2 to approval: ~29%.
- **Key valuation inputs:** Peak sales estimate (at what penetration rate), royalty/profit split if partnership exists, time to approval (DCF discount)
- **Comparable:** oncology CAR-T approvals (Kymriah, Yescarta, Breyanzi) as commercial benchmarks for market sizing

## Binary Risk: RF-15 (Product Concentration)

Single-asset pipelines are **inherently binary** — one Phase 3 failure = company potentially unviable. This is RF-15 and it always triggers in single-asset clinical stage companies. The question is whether the binary risk is compensated by the upside:
- Breakthrough Therapy / Fast Track = reduced binary risk (higher PoS)
- Differentiated MOA with clear Phase 2 data = reduced binary risk
- "Me-too" with no differentiation = PASS regardless of data

## PASS Signals in Biotech (from Exyn, Cortigent, others)

- **Going concern** (RF-01B) — burn rate vs. runway vs. clinical milestone timeline. If IPO proceeds + existing cash doesn't fund through next major clinical catalyst: PASS.
- **RF-08 (Management)** — first-time biotech CEO at Phase 2/3 company = serious red flag. Clinical execution requires experienced management.
- **RF-22 (Small firm)** — offering size <$50M on a Phase 2 clinical program rarely provides adequate runway. Institutional investors will not commit to programs that require a follow-on within 18 months.
- **RF-24 (Auditor quality)** — non-Big 4 on a clinical stage company with >$50M offering: flag.

## What Changes the Score Most in Biotech

The FHR score is almost entirely driven by cash runway analysis. The standard question:
- Does net IPO proceeds + existing cash fund the company through the **next two major clinical catalysts**?
- If yes: FHR can be 6.5-7.5
- If no (or if one catalyst): FHR likely 3.5-5.5
- If going concern: RF-01A or RF-01B applies; FHR likely 1.5-4.0
